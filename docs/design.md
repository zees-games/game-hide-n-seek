# The design

The family's fourth asymmetric game, after buses, deathrun and prop hunt, and the first where **the sides change during a round**: it starts as one or two against everybody and ends as everybody against one or two. Prop hunt's skill is choosing what to be; this one's is choosing where to be, and when to run. So the maps carry the game: every one has a way round (a chase that reaches a dead end is over), a way up, and a crawl space a seeker has to get down on their knees to look into.

Built from mg-prop-hunt, with the disguise, the hull, the hitboxes, the weapons and the decoy charge taken out and catching put in. Where a comment says another game found something, the line was kept because it is still true here.

## A catch is a measurement, and only the server makes one

Nothing is shot and nothing is claimed. Every tick of the seek, `HsGame._advance_catching` looks at where the server has every body:

- **A touch**: a seeker's capsule axis within `tag_range` (1.2 m; two bodies touching are 0.7) of a hider's, feet within 1.6 m of each other, and no wall between their middles.
- **Being watched**: the hider's head or chest inside a seeker's `sight_fov` cone within `sight_range`, nothing solid between (walls and furniture both). It fills a meter over `sight_seconds` that drains at `sight_decay` when nobody is looking.
- `catch_mode` picks touch (the default, as the brief asked), sight, or either.

A client sends movement and nothing else (`HsNetCommand` has no buttons of its own beyond the use key), so there is nothing a client could send to catch anybody.

**People do not collide with people**, as in prop hunt: a hider pinned in a corner by a seeker's body, or one blocking a doorway with their own, would be a different game, and a touch is a distance, so walking through costs nothing.

## A hider caught becomes a seeker, held still, on both ends

`HsGame.catch` moves the hider to the seekers where they stand (`caught_become_seekers`) and holds them for `caught_freeze_seconds` with the controller's `HOLD` modifier, the same one that blindfolds the seekers during the hide. **A modifier, not a timer the game checks**: it travels in the movement state, so the caught player's own client predicts them standing still on exactly the ticks the server holds them. With `caught_become_seekers` off, a caught hider is benched and watches (dot-spectate).

- **Where the caught start seeking** is `caught_seek_from` (`hs_caught_from`): 0, the default, where they were caught; 1, at the seekers' start. Measured with the stand-ins it made no difference to how many hiders are found ([bots](#bots)).
- **A joiner while the seekers are out seeks.** A hider dropped into a half-over round has had no time to hide; a seeker more is a round that ends a little sooner.
- **A fall is a catch.** A hider below `kill_y` during the seek is caught by it (`CAUGHT_FELL`) and stands up at the seekers' start; a seeker who falls is put back there.
- Health stays only for falls and for dot-moderation's slay, slap, god and buddha, which mean what they mean everywhere else. There are no hitboxes. dot-combat's trace is set anyway, or it warns at every boot that shots pass through walls.

## Few seekers, and the first one caught is next

`players_per_seeker` is 8 (one seeker up to eight players, two from nine), at most `max_seekers` 3, because the sides even themselves out as the round goes. `seeker_pick` defaults to `FIRST_CAUGHT`: whoever was caught first last round seeks, and `DotTeamRotation`'s random draw fills the rest. The other modes are dot-team's (RANDOM, QUEUE, SWAP); `!seek` / `!hide` / `!any` are wishes the draw honours, except over the first caught.

## The blindfold is true on the wire

As in prop hunt: the HUD paints a seeker's screen black during the hide, the `HOLD` modifier holds them still, and `net/hs_interest.gd` (`HsInterest`) sends a seeker's snapshots **no hider at all** until the seek starts. **The sight meter is the owner's alone** (`net_sight_meter` is `to_owner_only`), because a seeker told it would know which way to look.

## Everybody is themselves, and the side is the shirt

A hider is drawn as one of the family's animated people (dot-player-char's `DotHumanoidFigure`, the style every game's players, bots and NPCs share): the person their site avatar's blocky atlas letter names (dot-platform, `HsAvatars`, `DotHumanoidLook.from_letter`), with the top the side's colour outright: hiders blue, seekers orange. Not tinted: tinting a teal shirt blue read teal and a red one purple. The figure is told the floor, the vertical speed and the crouch, so a crouched hider is drawn crouching and a jump is drawn as one. A side change rebuilds the figure, so a caught hider turns orange on the next frame.

## Taunts, the beacon, points and stats

Taunts are prop hunt's (`DotPropTaunts`): hiders only, from the seek on, forced after `auto_taunt_seconds` (60) within `auto_taunt_radius`. The beacon marks every hidden hider when `beacon_hiders_left` (1) are left or `beacon_seconds_left` (0, off) remain, for `beacon_reveal` (2 s) in every `beacon_every` (10 s): a reveal the seekers run to and the hider runs from, not a marker held on them (held, every seeker walked to the last hider from anywhere and a round with one left was a round lost). The end-of-round beacon is off by default because it decided rounds the hiders had held: at medium the bots' hiders won 6 of 24 with it at 30 s, 10 without. Points: winning side 10, a catch 5, surviving 5, taunts. `HsProgress` keeps the stats (doors used among them) and the achievements.

## The HUD's glance pieces are dot-ui's

The clock is a `DotScoreStrip` with the hiders still hidden and the seekers as its two side plates, in the sides' colours, and "Hiders win in · <map>" under it (the blindfold's own clock uses `DotScoreStrip.clock_text`, so the two never disagree by a second). The door prompt under the crosshair is a `DotKeyPrompt`, "Open the toilet door" with the use key as bound drawn as a key; the sight meter sits under it, because a hider can have both up at once. The disc with the side's letter stays: nobody here has health, and the letter is the side. Colours from `HsHud.piece_theme()`. `tools/shot.sh --view=door` renders the prompt. Naming these classes makes the pack need a client shell with dot-ui API level 4; the derived `requires.json` asks for it and `headless_run` checks it.

## Props collide as their shape

Christian's first play test (2026-10-11) circled the trees wanting their low branches and met the canopy's box at arm's length all the way to the ground, and a bot pressed against that box looked like it hung in the air beside the tree. A prop now collides as its **pieces**: a few convex hulls measured out of the model by dot-props' `DotPropPieces` in `tools/measure_props.gd` (one decomposition per material, so a leaf clump and the trunk come apart), written to `props/pieces.json` and read by the server and every client alike, as the catalogue's sizes are. A tree is its trunk, its branch stubs and its leaf clumps; a table its top and legs, so a seeker sees under it; a sofa its seat, back and arms. A prop the decomposition cannot improve on (a fridge, a crate, anything under 0.6 m) keeps its box.

What a person can now do with Kenney's trees: walk in under the leaves to the trunk, hide behind it, and jump onto the stubby branches of the commonest tree (1.1 m and 1.5 m up, `headless_climb`). Kenney's clumps sit on their branches and the conifers are stacked cones, so no tree in the kit has a long limb to stand on, and two are made for it: `tools/climb_trees.py` (Blender, run headless; the GLBs in `assets/props/nature/` are its output) builds `climbtree_oak`, three limbs with flat tops climbing round the trunk at 1.2 m (a plain jump), 1.75 m (a crouch-jump from the ground) and 2.95 m, and `climbtree_fork`, a low limb at 1.25 m and a crotch at 1.6 m to stand in between two leaders. They are in the Nature Kit's style and its two material names (`woodBark`, `leafsGreen`), so the kit's recolour paints them like every other tree, authored in metres (`"climbtree_*": 1.0` in `props/kits.json`), with nothing solid within 1.9 m over any limb, so a person stands up on one; a leaf clump past each limb's tip is somewhere to hide behind up there. `headless_climb` jumps onto the oak's first limb, crouch-jumps onto its second and into the fork's crotch, and stands two seconds on each. Three stand in the park and three in the camp.

Measured on hs_camp (84 trees): 831 shapes became 2078, a server tick with twelve runners 1.04 ms became 1.05 ms, a ray 4.7 µs became 5.9 µs.

## Bots

Christian's first play test (2026-10-11): bot hiders stood together in the open and three were tagged inside one second; seekers ran in a cluster. The stand-ins (`game/hs_bots.gd`) are built on dot-bot now: its settings (`bot_difficulty` and the `bot_*` cvars, solo at the console too), its names (`DotBotNames` over `HsGame.BOT_NAMES`), its view turning (`DotBotAim`), and its hiding helpers.

- **The map is surveyed when it is built**, wherever bots run: `DotBotNav` bakes a walkable mesh from the walls, floors and furniture (not the doors: a door baked shut is a doorway the mesh does not cross, and a bot then never walks to it to open it), and `DotBotHiding.survey` scores about four hundred places (random points on the mesh and points beside props) by the share of eighty watchers' sight lines that reach a crouched head there. A quarter of a second of game time after the bake, always the same tick, so a seeded round replays the same.
- **A hider** picks a hidden place apart from the others (12 m: a hider caught becomes a seeker where it stands, next to anybody hidden near it), away from the seekers' start, that it can reach before the seekers go, deeper down the list the less `bot_map_knowledge`; it walks the mesh's path, crouches under low ceilings, opens doors, and keeps still crouched there facing the way it came. It runs (sprinting, to somewhere out of that seeker's sight at least 12 m from it, never past it) from a seeker that can see it, one within 2.4 m, and, while the beacon shows it, one it sees or hears. A seeker walking at its place without having seen it, or a catch close by, sends it away low and quiet only when that seeker could not see it if it looked; in its sight line it keeps still, because moving is what would be seen (`headless_bots`' trace: hiders that ran from a seeker coming their way were caught 29 times in 30). Through a doorway with a seeker behind it, or into a room it is about to hide in, it stops, turns and shuts the door (a shut door is a wall to a seeker's eye and costs it the swing to open). It shuffles to a nearby place before the game's standing-still meter forces a taunt; it taunts of its own accord about once in five minutes and only when no seeker has been near for half a minute.
- **Nav annotations replace the random survey for hiding** (dot-npc-ai's `DotNpcAiNavAnnotator`, stepped a fixed number of slices per tick so a replay is the same, cached under `user://nav/`): hiders choose from `hiding_spots()` weighted by enclosure and by crouched cover from the seekers' start (`cover_from`), spread apart; seekers tour the top spots and chokepoints nearest-first, then sweep a random survey. Drop and jump-up links are added to the mesh as rides and a seeker hops at a jump-up foot. `headless_bots` holds that the hiders' places beat the old survey's by `MIN_QUALITY_GAIN` and that no map is unwinnable. A bot that gives up its goal at a door shuns the corner it was walking to for 30 s (`SHUN_*`), so it does not pick a place straight back through the panel it could not pass.
- **A seeker** searches with `DotBotSearch`: likely places first (as far as `bot_map_knowledge` goes), near first, crossing off what it has had in view, never where another seeker is going; footsteps it hears (running, sprinting, much less crouched) and taunts put the places round them first. It searches at a walk (`SEARCH_PACE`, 0.28 of a run, and the same pace crouched: under a cabin it crawled at a third of that) and chases at a sprint; a place it has stood on is crossed off even if its eye never had it in view. It sees a hider only with its own cone, `bot_sight` and the game's sight test: somebody keeping still is made out at half the distance, still and crouched at 0.10 of it and only whole (head and chest), after a moment longer; within 2 m a body is felt. It notices after `bot_reaction`, chases at a sprint turning at `bot_turn_rate`, and looks where the hider went for `bot_memory`. The beacon on the last hiders is the only thing it is told.
- **An open door's panel across the way** (the path is baked without doors) is walked round by its free edge (`_door_detour`); with no room round it the bot shuts it and the walk opens it again from its own side. **A door that would swing into the bot itself** (pinned with `away: false` to open towards it, or an open panel whose shutting arc it stands in) is never pressed from there, because the game refuses a use while anybody, the opener too, is in the swing: the bot steps back out of the arc, facing the door, and presses from there (`_plan_open_from_clear`, `_plan_shut_from_clear`, `_open_from_clear`); a park cabin's inward front door held a fleeing hider eighteen seconds before. A bot still round one spot beside an open panel after 5 s shuts it (`_linger_by_door`), and after 8 s goes somewhere else; twice round the same panel within 10 s is somewhere else too. Out of the swing and pressing for a second with the door not moving is somebody else in its way: it gives up, and leaves that door alone for 10 s (`_shut_failed`; forgotten at every give-up, the same step back was planned every three seconds). A way round planned while the panel was still swinging is planned again once it moves. A bot held still (a new seeker's freeze) is not walking, so it is neither stuck nor lingering.
- **A door counts only on the bot's own storey** (`_door_on_level`: its foot within a step of the bot's feet, its head above a crouched body): a hider in the crawl space under a camp cabin went round the panel in the room over its head for fourteen seconds.
- **A corner is passed only once the way to the next is open to a body** (`_body_clear`, three lines at a rail's two bar heights, walls and furniture but not doors, which are `_door_detour`'s): passed early, a bot turning round the end of the manor gallery's rail walked into its end post. Counting the doors too, a corner on an open panel's edge was never passed, because a body cannot come within reach of it.
- **Caught on a doorway's frame on the way through**, the first answer to being stuck is a way square to the doorway's middle and through (`_through_doorway`); only once stuck, because steering every path through every doorway's middle sent bots round in circles elsewhere. No hop under a low ceiling (a crawl space). Stepping round people deflects a bot sideways and never back: two bots meeting in a doorway pushed each other out of it for sixteen seconds.
- **Stuck** (no headway for 0.6 s, on the ground or hung on an edge in the air neither rising nor falling: a bot pressed into a cabin's jamb at the top of its steps hung there half a minute), a bot hops (never at something waist-high: that is a desk), then steps aside, then picks somewhere else; the count resets only once it is 1.5 m from where it first stuck, because a step aside and back is not headway. A seeker's abandoned place is crossed off. A hider's shuffle before a forced taunt is to a place within 18 m of walking, not 14 m in a straight line through a wall. It steps round anybody within a metre, because people pass through people here and a bot walking through somebody walks through their camera.

**Measured** 2026-10-13 after bots stopped lingering at doors (`hns-bot-door-linger-2`: doors on their own storey only, corners passed once the way on is open, a doorway's middle when stuck on its frame, a failed shut remembered, sideways round people, somewhere else after 8 s; maps: small rooms' doors slide, crowded doorways cleared), search pace 0.28, still-and-crouched sight 0.10: at medium the hiders win 11 of 24 rounds on seeds 0-23 and 11 of 24 on 24-47, 18% and 24% of hiders are never found, and the longest a stand-in lingers beside an open panel is 9.8 s and 9.0 s (12.7 s and 14.6 s before, with the toys in). The suite holds it to 10 s.

**Measured before that**, 2026-10-12, after bots stepped out of a door's swing to use it and the maps lost their panels with no way round (`hns-bot-door-linger-1`): at medium the hiders win 11 of 24 rounds on seeds 0-23 (15 of 24 on 24-47), 20% and 30% of hiders are never found, and the longest a stand-in lingers beside an open panel is 9.8 s on seeds 0-23 and 14.5 s on 24-47 (18 s and 15 s before). The suite holds it to 15 s. Two identical runs give identical rounds. Before that, after bots walked round open door panels (`hns-bot-door-corner-1`), search pace 0.30: 11 of 24 (9 on the next 24), 23% and 19% never found, 18 s and 15 s at worst (122 s on round 23 alone before).

**Measured earlier** (`examples/headless_bots`, seven stand-ins on six maps, one seeker by the default draw, 240 s rounds, 24 rounds, deterministic), 2026-10-12, after dot-player-controller stopped seekers hanging in the corners behind open doors (which had taken the hiders from 12 rounds in 24 to 6, so the still-and-crouched sight went from 0.18 to 0.12): at medium a hider lasts 130 s on average, 18% are never found, the hiders win 11 rounds in 24 (8 on the next 24 seeds) and the seekers catch somebody in 24; at easy 166 s, 41%, 18 of 24; at hard 121 s, 12%, 8 of 24. Hiders shut about six doors a round. The suite holds medium to 30-60% of rounds and 15-40% of hiders. Before (2026-10-11's stand-ins): 88 s, 4% never found, 2 rounds in 20, because hiders crept or ran from every seeker coming their way and were caught doing it (29 of 30 that ran from one walking at them), seekers searched at a run and saw a still, crouched hider at a quarter of their sight with only its head showing, and the last half minute's beacon was held on everybody left. Where a caught hider starts seeking was measured too (`hs_caught_from`): starting at the seekers' start instead of where caught changed nothing (9% never found against 10%; 16% of catches were by a new seeker within 15 m of where it was caught), so the rule stays where caught. Surveying a map takes 110 to 300 ms on the server, once per map.

## Toys

Christian's first play test asked for things to play with on every map ("a large beachball players can shoot and hit that'll bounce around ... that's just one example"). They are dot-props' toy catalogue, so every game in the family gets the same set from the same data, and each map's are chosen for the place in hide-n-seek-maps' builder (`toys_<map>`): a swing, a see-saw, a roundabout and a trampoline in the park and the school's playground, the school's bell, the ship's bell and horn on its forward deck, the castle's skittles and herald's horn, the camp's dinner bell and obstacle course, the village's fountain, lamps on a switch along paths.

- **The server's physics, the client's picture.** `HsToyNet` replicates each toy's moving part's transform, whether it is on, and a use counter; a client's toys are frozen and posed from it, and play their sound when the counter moves. Paired by index: both ends build the same toys from the same document, so TOYS (after MAP, and to a joiner after theirs) is the net ids in order and the map's id. **A client registers every mirror the moment TOYS arrives**, under a holder of the bridge's, and pairs them with its own toys when that map is built here: dot-net stops reading a snapshot at the first entity a client does not know, so a client whose MAP never arrived (past a browser socket's buffer, or a format behind) read no snapshot to its end while the mirrors waited for the map (`headless_net`, armed). A released toy entity leaves the tree at once as well as the registry.
- **A person is not a rigid body.** A ball, a pin, a barrel or a tyre is on the `toy` layer, which people do not collide with: walking into one is `HsGame._advance_toys` pushing it ahead of you, a little faster than you walk. So a ball is never a wall, and a bot can never be stuck on one; the bots walk through them and push them like anybody. A swing, a see-saw, a roundabout or a platform is on `prop`, solid, and the bots' walkable mesh goes round its footprint.
- **The use key takes a toy looked at before a door**, by a ray from the eyes: kick, push, spin, punch, press, honk, ring. The prompt under the crosshair names it ("Kick the football").
- **A roundabout carries whoever stands on it** round its hub: `HsController._on_post_simulate` asks the toys (`HsGame._carry`, dot-props' `carry_for`) on every end that simulates the person, so a predicting client goes round with the deck it is shown, by the spin it measures from the poses (the spin is not sent), and the correction is small. A rotation about the hub, so a rider a metre out stays a metre out. The view is not turned: a command's yaw is the player's.
- **A trampoline or a bounce pad is movement, not physics**: `HsController._on_pre_simulate` asks the toys for a launch on every end that simulates the person, so a predicting client is thrown on the tick the server throws it; only the server counts the bounce (`bounced` → `DotPropToyBox.launched`), which is what everybody hears. The builder keeps both clear of anything their flight could land a person on.
- **Balance.** A toy is cover (the sight test sees the `toy` layer: crouch behind a barrel), never a hiding place nobody can reach. Every toy goes home at the top of a round, so a ball kicked into last round's hiding place points at nothing. During the hide a seeker's client is told nothing about the toys (`HsInterest`), because a ball rolling away from a hider's path is that hider on the wire. `headless_bots` still holds its bands with the toys in (the hiders won 12 of 24 rounds, 22% never found, 12.7 s the longest by a panel).
- **Cost.** At most 24 loose toys simulate at once, 8 in a browser (`sv_toys_awake`); the rest sleep. The driven ones (swing, see-saw, roundabout, platform, bag) are an angle each, not joints. A map has 9 to 19 toys.

## Weapons were taken out

Prop hunt's weapon plumbing (zee-dot-weapons, `ARMED`, the input's slot) was removed rather than left idle. Seeker items found on a map, which the brief anticipates, would copy it back from mg-prop-hunt.

## Colour and light

The family's sunny look (game-arena's built-in maps), carried over from mg-prop-hunt, whose `ph_materials.gd` and `ph_map.gd` these files mirror.

- **Every building surface** (floor, wall, ceiling, roof, the ground outside) is Kenney's light prototype grid, or his tiles on a floor, under a pastel per surface (`HsMaterials.PASTELS`: peach wood, periwinkle carpet, coral brick, mint grass, lavender roof), mapped in world space at 4 m a copy. A map's tint still multiplies it, paled halfway to white, because the tints were chosen against the old darker colours and at full strength turned a pastel to mud. Glass, water, court lines, boards, lockers, metal, fabric and lamps keep their generated look; furniture keeps Kenney's colours, so a hider crouched behind a sofa is beside a coloured thing in a pale room. The two textures load through `HsPaths.rebase` and fall back to the generated look when absent.
- **Light is a dot-lighting document made from the map's `environment`** (`HsMap.light_document`): the map's sun angle and colour, its sky at the zenith and under the horizon, its horizon as fog from 120 m (haze at most 0.25); brightness 380 times the square of `sun_energy`. Glow starts above white (the lamps), not at dot-lighting's 0.85, where a room of pale walls glowed milky.
- **It is drawn with dot-lighting's `painted()`** (`HsMap.light_profile`, as mg-prop-hunt's `PhMap`): light matched to the renderer (Compatibility, this game's and the shell's, clips a sunlit pastel to white at Forward+'s numbers), a lower exposure, a grade, and a shadowless fill from the side the sun is not on, re-aimed after the map's own sun angle is set, or it lights the faces the sun already does. The ambient is the map's `ambient_energy` times 4 of `painted()`'s and the fill times 2.8, so a room, lit by the fill and its lamps rather than the sun, stays as light as before; game-arena's pale cool fill at its flat 1.0 had turned every room into a lavender haze with nothing in shadow. Contrast is 1.04, not `painted()`'s 1.12: the grade pivots at mid grey, and 1.12 put Kenney's dark trunks and furniture in shade at black. Shadows stay on in the browser.
- **The ground outside (grass, concrete, sidewalk, tiles, dirt, sand, asphalt) is a step deeper than the walls**: it is half of every outdoor frame, and at near white a sunlit field rendered at 0.9 luminance and over. Measured under Compatibility (seeker view, 95th percentile of luminance), park 0.92 to 0.84, school 0.92 to 0.85, village 0.90 to 0.87, camp 0.83 to 0.76, with at most 0.3% of a frame under 0.03. Check a look change in both renderers and look at the near-black share, not only the blown one.
- **A contact shade where boxes meet** (`HsMap._add_contact_shade`, dot-lighting's `DotContactShade`, API 4, by path behind `DotAddonApi.build_level`): soft bands at a wall's foot and head, in a room's corners and under a counter, which SSAO gives on Forward+ and Compatibility cannot. Every visible opaque box casts and takes one; glass and water neither. **Furniture: only blocks** (`HsMap._shades_floor`): a furniture-kit prop that collides as its own box (no `pieces`) and stands upright (a cabinet, a counter, a fridge, a bookcase, a washer: 487 of the 2 032 placed in the eleven maps) casts and takes none. A piece-built prop (a chair, a table, a tree) is open underneath, so its bounding box would shade floor that is lit. Nothing here breaks and the furniture is one static body, so the shade (one mesh built at load) never outlives what cast it; doors and secret panels move and are not in it. mg-prop-hunt makes the same call and also leaves out its breakable furniture. Rendered the apartments' kitchen before and after.

## Cameras and bodies

From the first play test (a stand-in walking into the camera at 1:37, a round ending on a face filling the screen at 2:20, the camera inside tree canopies at 0:33): the third-person camera is a dot-core `DotCameraBoom` swept from the eye with `CHASE_UP` as `lift` (a pivot above the head was inside the canopy), stopping against the house and the furniture; a `DotCameraFade` on the camera is handed every other player and hides one at the lens, fades one between the third-person camera and you. F5 goes through the server's `mp_forceview` (dot-player-controller), bound REPLICATED and followed through the link; dot-spectate is handed the level for its chase and death cameras. People pass through people by default here (`mp_solidplayers 0`, `HsGame._init`): the layout's reason (pinning a hider in a corner, blocking a doorway) still holds, and it was measured: with bodies solid the stand-in hiders lost 6 rounds of 6 in `headless_bots`. An owner who sets 1 gets a motor mask and hull per player. `tools/camera_check.sh [--render]` flies both cameras round the house (armed), checks the rule, and draws both views and the walk-in before and after.


## Still loading: nobody is caught or drawn behind a cover

A client behind its loading cover froze itself and cannot see or run, so the server must not act on it. `DotGameModule` knows who is loading (`game.cover` from `DotGameClient`); the module hands the world `HsGame.loading_fn(key)`. `_advance_catching` skips a covered hider (no touch, no sight, the meter rests) and does not let a covered seeker catch; `_draw_sides` draws seekers from the loaded seats while enough of them exist (otherwise everybody). `catch()` itself stays open, so an admin's `hs_catch` and a fall still work. A covered seat counts as alive. Unset (offline, bots, every suite but `dedicated`) nobody is loading. `dedicated` checks it over a real socket and `tools/render_cover.tscn` renders the cover and the frames after it lifts.

## Free roam for whoever is out

On by default: the person who is out presses G (a binding, `hs_roam`) and flies a free camera with the movement keys, Sprint for fast, Walk for slow, the mouse to look; G again goes back to a player. `mp_freeroam 0` turns it off, and `mp_forcecamera 1` or `2` does too, because a camera that goes anywhere cannot be held to one side. The rules are one `DotSpectatorRules` (`HsGame.spectate_rules`), shared with the manager, bound as replicated cvars by the module and followed by the client. `hs_spectate_camera` (the config's `spectate_camera`) only sets `mp_forcecamera` and is kept for old server configs.

**The server owns the mode and the client flies the camera.** The key is a `ROAM` request; the server's `toggle_roam` decides, refuses with a notice ("Free roam is off on this server"), and the `SPECTATE` view event carries `ROAMING` back like any other mode. The per-frame pose is the client's own (`DotSpectatorFreeCam`, seeded from where the camera was), never on the wire. An operator who tightens the rules mid-roam puts the roamer back on a player on the next tick.
